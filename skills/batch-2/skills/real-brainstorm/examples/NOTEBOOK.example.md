## the wrong instinct

<When a note sounds weak, almost everyone does the same thing — they lean in. More arm weight, more index finger, more pressure.>
<video clip of a player's right index finger visibly pressing down into the frog mid-note, tight shot on the hand only, no face #technique-broll>
<show image of a hand-drawn arrow labelled "MORE" pointing straight down into a string, crude and deliberate, black marker on white>

## what actually makes the sound

Slow this down. This is the explanation beat and it should feel calm after the
hook. One idea per visual, no rapid cutting.

<The string doesn't care how hard you push it. It cares how much of it you set moving, and how fast.>
<show image of a single string vibrating in a wide arc, side view, motion blurred at the belly of the arc, dark background, the width of the arc obvious>
<Bow speed is what makes a note ring out into the room. Pressure only decides how hard the string grips the hair before it releases.>
<show image of a rosin-and-hair contact point magnified, hair grabbing and releasing the string, drawn as a simple diagram with two labelled arrows: "grip" and "release">
<Too much grip and too little speed, and the string never gets to vibrate freely. That's the crushed, airless sound you're hearing.>
<show image of the same vibrating-string frame as before, same framing and colours, but the arc collapsed to almost nothing — the difference has to be obvious at a glance>
<caption: no room to vibrate>

## the fix

<So the next time it sounds thin, don't press harder. Move faster. Full bow, same weight, and let the string do the work.>
<video clip of a full-length bow stroke frog to tip, filmed from the player's own point of view over the right shoulder, one unbroken take, arm travelling the whole width of frame ~4s #technique-broll>
<caption: same weight. more bow.>
<It will feel like you're doing less. You are. That's the point.>
<show image of a player's face relaxing mid-note, eyes closed, soft side light — reaction, not demonstration ?>
