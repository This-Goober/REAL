## hook

White page, nothing but the grid. The caption is the only other thing on screen.

<Let's play a guessing game. I'm thinking of a number from one to one hundred.>
<show image of the numbers one to one hundred as a ten by ten grid of small gray cells on a white page, the range written quietly beneath it #img-grid>
<caption: guess my number>

## the slow way

The ticking has to feel slow and endless; it must not finish.

<You could guess one, two, three, four. That could take a hundred tries.>
<video clip of the same grid with the numbers being crossed out one at a time from the top left while a tries counter climbs beneath it, never finishing, white page #clip-one-by-one>
<caption: one at a time is slow>

## the trick

<Here is the trick. Guess the middle, fifty. Too high? Then throw away all the bigger numbers.>
<video clip of the grid with the cell for fifty ringed, the words too high appearing beneath, then every number above fifty fading to pale gray, white page #clip-halve>
<caption: guess the middle>

## again

<Guess the middle of what is left. Twenty five. Too low? Throw away the smaller ones.>
<show image of the grid with the numbers above fifty already pale, the cell for twenty five ringed, the words too low beneath, and the numbers below twenty five pale as well #img-again>
<caption: half again>

## payoff

Let the staircase sit after the last word.

<Every guess cuts the pile in half. Seven guesses and you have found it. That is binary search.>
<show image of a column of gray bars each half the width of the one above, labelled one hundred, fifty, twenty five, thirteen, seven, four, two, one, with seven guesses written beneath #img-halving>
<title: Binary search>
