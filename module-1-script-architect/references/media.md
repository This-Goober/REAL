# Media ideas — the bar

Every scene gets 1–3 proposals. They exist so the director can go find or shoot
material without re-deriving what each moment needs. They are **advisory** —
the director owns every content decision, and the plan says so.

## The four tags

| Tag | When | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| `FILM` | the director has to shoot it | framing, action, and **how much usable footage to get** (always more than the scene needs) |
| `FIND` | it exists online | 2 search phrases + where to look |
| `MAKE` | it can be generated | what it shows + how (matplotlib, music21, text overlay, screenshot) |
| `OWN` | probably already in the director's folder | the likely filename or asset-catalog entry |

Check `OWN` first. If `asset-cataloger` has run on the folder, its catalog tells
you what exists — proposing a `FIND` for something the director already shot
wastes their time and reads as inattention.

## What makes an idea good

**Specific enough to act on.** "Something about waves" is useless. "Two sine waves
drifting out of phase, collision points flashing" is a thing the director can go
make.

**Tied to the words.** The image has to match what is being said at that moment.
Relevance beats production value; a plain diagram on the right word beats a
beautiful one on the wrong word, and covering a gap with a mismatched leftover is
worse than leaving it blank (Principle 10).

**Honest about effort.** A `FILM` item costs a shooting session. If a `FIND` or a
`MAKE` would land the same idea, offer it as the alternate on the same scene and
say which you would pick.

**Sized to the slot.** A 1.5s scene cannot hold a busy diagram. Match the
complexity of the proposal to the seconds available — that is why every shot-list
line carries its scene's duration.

## Writing search terms

Two phrases, different in kind, not two phrasings of one:

- one **literal** — what the thing is called ("microtonal keyboard")
- one **vernacular** — how people who post it label it ("piano keys pitch slider")

Name where: `stock` (Pexels/Unsplash), `Wikimedia`, `YouTube` (for motion), `meme`
(Know Your Meme / Giphy), `paper figure` (for the real diagram). Different sources
want different phrasings, and saying which one you mean saves a round trip.

## Writing a FILM spec

Three parts, one line: **framing · action · usable length**.

> POV, office, drone audible — play the shift slightly flat, then react. ~10s usable.

Always ask for more footage than the scene needs. A 4s scene wants 10s shot, so
the cut point can be chosen rather than accepted. If the shot has an exit frame
that matters (the bow coming down, the thumbs up), say which frame it is.

## Writing a MAKE spec

Say what it shows *and* how it gets built, because "make a diagram" is a whole
afternoon and "same matplotlib call, two datasets, identical axes" is ten minutes.
Reuse is the highest-value thing you can notice: if scene 26 is scene 19's figure
with a different phase, saying so turns two jobs into one.

Recurring MAKE types in this series: matplotlib waveform plots, music21 staff
renders, single-word text cards in the series style, app screenshots.

## What you never do here

Do not source, download, or generate the media. Do not pick between options for
them. Do not silently swap a proposal into a later revision because you preferred
it. The proposals go in the plan tagged as proposals, and the director answers by
scene ID.
